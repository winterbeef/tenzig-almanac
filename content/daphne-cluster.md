## From a Settlement Prospectus, Daphne System Colonial Development Office

Yakiyah (0605) offers prospective settlers arable land, a stable climate, and — the Office is pleased to note — none of the resource disputes currently troubling neighboring systems. Early survey teams report the local mineral wealth modest but reliable, sufficient to support a self-sufficient colony within two generations without external subsidy.

The Office notes with some pride that Yakiyah has attracted interest from several devotional communities seeking a quiet world for uninterrupted worship and study, and recommends the system favorably to any settler group prioritizing stability over ambition.

> **Corvid:** I do not know which devotional community this prospectus is politely declining to name, nor whether it is the ancestor of anything that governs Yakiyah in the present era. What I can say is that "quiet world for uninterrupted worship and study" is not a phrase I would ever have guessed applies, six centuries later, to the seat of a mega-corporation with a weapons manufacturing arm and a theocratic command structure.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Every quiet devotional community I've ever delivered cargo to eventually turned out to be sitting on either a fortune or a secret. Usually both. I don't know why anyone's still surprised by this pattern at this point in history.

## From a Merchant's Travel Diary, Kos, District of Arnorun

Arrived in Arnorun after nine days' transit and found it, to my genuine surprise, one of the liveliest districts I have traded in all cycle — a proper working city, dense with fabrication houses, a market that runs three shifts around the clock, and a research quarter that half the district seems inordinately proud of without being willing to explain precisely what it researches.

Asked a fabrication-house foreman what went on up at the research quarter. He said, with a straight face, "Good things, mostly," and would not elaborate further no matter how many rounds I bought him. I have decided I like Arnorun very much and intend to trade here again.

> **Corvid:** this entry is, of everything in this volume, the one I find hardest to sit with. The present-day record of Arnorun's "research quarter" describes it only as the Graves — ruins of vast pre-Scream scale, said to hold priceless knowledge, dangerous enough that few venture in and fewer come out with anything to show for it. This merchant walked those same streets while they were a living, working, proud city, ate in restaurants that are now rubble, and complained good-naturedly about a foreman who wouldn't gossip about his own workplace.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've stood at the edge of the Graves. I didn't go in, and I've turned down good money to go in, twice. Reading this — a whole working city, a market running three shifts, someone buying a foreman a round of drinks — that's the first time it's ever felt like a place instead of a rumor. I don't love how that feels.
>
> **Cinder:** A working city — three shifts a day, fabrication houses, a foreman who could afford to buy a stranger drinks — doesn't just become "the Graves" on its own. Somebody's research quarter did that to somebody's home, and the histories file it under ruins instead of asking who was running the quarter and who signed off on whatever it was building toward. Read the [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Eurymedon Concern's own memo]] elsewhere in this book if you want to see exactly what that kind of quiet, well-funded secrecy looks like while it's still being written down. It's never the workers who get to explain what happened to them. It's always whoever owned the building.
>
> **Two-Coin:** No argument from me on that one.

## From an Internal Yakiyah Devotional Circular, Author Unrecorded

Brothers and sisters newly arrived to our world: know that the labor asked of you here in service of the Circle's ongoing study is neither punishment nor mere employment, but a sacred trust extended to few. Those selected for the deeper stages of the work are asked, as always, to hold their participation in confidence even from family, for the Circle's methods — though gentle, though carefully overseen — are not yet ready for wider understanding, and premature disclosure serves no one.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I include this circular with real reluctance, because I cannot prove it connects to anything, and because the phrase "deeper stages of the work" is doing a great deal of unexplained labor in that sentence. Given everything else this volume touches on regarding the years before the Scream, I did not feel right leaving it out.
>
> **Two-Coin:** "Not yet ready for wider understanding" is a phrase I have heard exactly twice in my life, both times right before something went very badly for the people it was said about. I don't say that to be dramatic. I say it because it's true.
