## Overview

*Mandate-era summary.* Yakiyah offers arable land, stable climate, and modest but reliable mineral wealth; several devotional communities have petitioned for settlement rights, unnamed at their own request pending formal registry. Kos's Arnorun district is a dense fabrication and research hub, three-shift industrial operation, no irregularities found on initial survey. Recommend routine follow-up on Yakiyah's devotional settlement petitions once registry is complete.

## Dispatch — Merchant's Travel Account, Kos, District of Arnorun

```
FIELD DISPATCH (RECOVERED, PRE-SCREAM)
Transmission: recovered, not direct
```

Arrived in Arnorun after nine days' transit and found it, to my genuine surprise, one of the liveliest districts I have traded in all cycle — a proper working city, dense with fabrication houses, a market that runs three shifts around the clock, and a research quarter that half the district seems inordinately proud of without being willing to explain precisely what it researches.

Asked a fabrication-house foreman what went on up at the research quarter. He said, with a straight face, "Good things, mostly," and would not elaborate further no matter how many rounds I bought him. I have decided I like Arnorun very much and intend to trade here again.

*— unsigned, undated*

> **[FIELD NOTE — M. Okonkwo-Reyes, Archive Adept, 2nd Rank, 3176.090]**
> This is, of everything in this volume, the fragment I find hardest to sit with. The present-day record of Arnorun's "research quarter" describes it only as the Graves — ruins of vast pre-Scream scale, said to hold priceless knowledge, dangerous enough that few venture in and fewer come out with anything to show for it. This merchant walked those same streets while they were a living, working, proud city, and complained good-naturedly about a foreman who wouldn't gossip about his own workplace.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Nine-Finger Sable, smuggler (self-described), 3140.303]**
> I've stood at the edge of the Graves. Turned down good money to go in, twice. Reading the account above — a whole working city, a foreman buying a stranger drinks — that's the first time it's ever felt like a place instead of a rumor to me. Don't love how that feels.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Cor. Dassin, Cerberus Corps field agent, 3188.169]**
> A working city — three shifts a day, fabrication houses, a foreman who could afford to buy a stranger drinks — doesn't just become "the Graves" on its own. Somebody's research quarter did that to somebody's home. Read the [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Eurymedon Concern's own memo]] elsewhere in this volume if you want to see what that kind of quiet, well-funded secrecy looks like while it's still being written down. It's never the workers who get to explain what happened to them.
