---
title: "The Unmarked Departure"
---

A colony ship passes through the cluster bound for a destination no chart names — and nobody agrees on where she came from, either. Every other entry in this volume concerns a place. This one concerns a ship, and there is no tidy account of her to give. What follows are contradictions: several separate fragments, from several separate sources, each confident about where she came from, and no two of them agreeing. All are printed rather than resolved, because resolving them would mean pretending to a certainty nobody involved in compiling this actually has.

## Public Registry Notice, Perope Cluster Shipping Authority

```
MANDATE RECORD — SHIPPING TRANSIT NOTICE
Coordinates:   0404 (Perope cluster, transit only)
Vessel:        registered under standard settlement charter, name redacted at filing
Last Verified: 2611.301
```

Notice is hereby given of the transit, this cycle, of the colony vessel registered under a standard settlement charter, inbound from beyond the cluster's charted edge and outbound toward a destination filed under seal at the request of the sponsoring concern, said sponsor citing "commercial sensitivity" as grounds for the seal. The Authority confirms the vessel has passed all required safety inspections and continues transit with full crew, full colonist complement, and standard consumables loadout for an extended onward journey.

The Authority notes, for the curious, that a colony charter arriving already this far into an unlogged transit, sealed at both ends, is, in the Authority's considerable experience, quite rare.

> **[FIELD NOTE — J. Praast, Mandate Cartographic Office (ret.), 3134.053]**
> "Inbound from beyond the cluster's charted edge" is as much as this document is willing to say, and it is maddeningly unspecific — it could mean she came from three systems over or three hundred. I believe, though I stress this only as belief, that this concerns the same vessel discussed throughout this entry.

## Dispatch — Dockworker's Diary, Likely [[avire|Avire]]

```
FIELD DISPATCH (RECOVERED, PRE-SCREAM)
Location:      waystation along the Perope corridor, likely Avire
Transmission:  recovered
```

Loaded the last of the crates myself, on account of half my crew calling in sick, which I now suspect was less illness and more good sense on their part, though I couldn't tell you why I suspect that. Strange ship. Ordinary enough to look at — colony hull, standard rotation drum, nothing you wouldn't see on a dozen other charters — but the loading manifest didn't match what actually went into the hold, not by my count, and the foreman told me twice not to worry about the discrepancy, which is exactly the number of times it takes me to start worrying about something.

Talked to one of her crew while we worked, briefly, before an officer called him off. Asked him, casual as I could manage, how long they'd been underway. He said "longer than you'd believe," and something in the way he said it made me not want to press further. Didn't get a straight answer on where she'd started from.

Watched her burn out-system that evening, still heading further out, not toward any of the settled approaches I recognized.

*— unsigned*

> **[FIELD NOTE — Sella Voth, Guild of Pilots escort captain, 3151.293]**
> Old Guild hands will tell you, with total confidence, that she came coreward — out past Rado, out past anything on a common chart, somewhere in toward the old worlds proper. Others will tell you, with equal confidence, that "coreward" is just what people say when they mean "I don't actually know and don't want to admit it." I lean toward believing she came from a long way off. I don't lean toward believing anyone who claims to know exactly how far.

## Three Competing Accounts of Her Origin

**The first account**, carried in Guild of Pilots oral tradition among [[perope|Perope]] cluster escort crews, holds that she departed from somewhere deep coreward — a world or shipyard near the old heart of things, long before anyone in this cluster ever laid eyes on her, and that her transit through Perope and Tabet space was only the last, smallest leg of a much longer journey whose true starting point nobody here ever charted.

```
UNVERIFIED INTELLIGENCE LOG
Reliability:   secondhand, multiply corroborated within the Guild
Location:      Perope corridor
Source:        Guild of Pilots oral tradition
Logged:        3151.293 (this recording)
```

**The second account**, told on [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Hypsila]] among guildhouse traders, holds that she was never coreward at all, but built and launched from somewhere much closer — a quiet, well-funded operation the traders can never quite name, and that the "coreward" story is a deliberate misdirection, planted by whoever paid for her passage to keep the curious looking in the wrong direction entirely.

```
UNVERIFIED INTELLIGENCE LOG
Reliability:   secondhand
Location:      Hypsila guildhouses
Source:        trader chatter, unattributed
Logged:        3122.024
```

**The third account**, the rarest of the three, holds that she had no single point of departure at all — that she had been underway, in one form or another, for so long before anyone in this cluster saw her that the question "where did she come from" stopped having a meaningful answer generations before her crew ever hailed a Perope dockworker.

```
UNVERIFIED INTELLIGENCE LOG
Reliability:   unverified, single source
Location:      unrecorded
Source:        unnamed traveler, retold at least twice before recording
Logged:        3176.093
```

> **[FIELD NOTE — Cor. Dassin, Cerberus Corps field agent, 3188.172]**
> I favor the second account, and not because it's the most interesting — because it's the most familiar. Quiet money, an unlogged launch, a "commercial sensitivity" seal slapped over the whole business before anyone downstream could ask a real question. I've watched concerns and consortiums pull exactly this trick on a dozen worlds. Claiming she came from somewhere impossibly far is always cheaper than admitting whose backyard she actually left from.

> **[FIELD NOTE — M. Okonkwo-Reyes, Archive Adept, 2nd Rank, 3176.093]**
> For what it's worth, I logged the third account myself, and I don't fully believe it either. I include it because the man who told it to me did not seem to be lying, only repeating something he had never managed to make sense of. That seemed worth preserving on its own terms.

## Letter, Author and Recipient Both Unrecorded

They tell me the posting is an honor, and perhaps it is, but I confess I do not entirely understand what it is I have volunteered for, only that the pay is extraordinary and the confidentiality clause longer than any I have signed before. I am told the work is important — vital, even, to "solving a problem that has troubled us for generations" — and that I should be proud to be chosen for it.

I am proud. I am also, if I am honest with this page and no one else, a little afraid, though I could not tell you of what, precisely. Perhaps that is simply what important work feels like, from the inside, before you understand it well enough to know if the fear is warranted.

*[hand unknown, undated]*

> **[FIELD NOTE — Nine-Finger Sable, smuggler (self-described), 3140.305]**
> Some doors you don't knock on even in a book nobody living can be blamed for. I've wondered more than once whether this was written by someone who boarded that ship. I've decided I'd rather not know for certain.
