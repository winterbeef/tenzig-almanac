## A Word Before the Fragments

> **Corvid:** I want to be honest about this entry before you read it, rather than after. Every other entry in this volume concerns a place. This one concerns a ship — the vessel later remembered, by those few who remember it at all, as the *Dusk's Long Shadow* — and I do not have a tidy account of her to give you. I have contradictions. I have three separate fragments, from three separate sources, each confident about where she came from, and no two of them agree. I have chosen to print all three rather than pick a favorite, because picking a favorite would mean pretending to certainty I don't have.
>
> **Two-Coin:** For what it's worth, I've heard a fourth version that isn't even written down here, from a Guild pilot who swore his grandfather's log placed her passage a full decade earlier than any of this. I didn't include it because I couldn't verify a word of it. Corvid's right to print the contradictions instead of smoothing them over. I'd rather sit with "we don't know" than read a clean lie.

## From a Public Registry Notice, Perope Cluster Shipping Authority

Notice is hereby given of the transit, this cycle, of the colony vessel registered under a standard settlement charter, inbound from beyond the cluster's charted edge and outbound toward a destination filed under seal at the request of the sponsoring concern, said sponsor citing "commercial sensitivity" as grounds for the seal. The Authority confirms the vessel has passed all required safety inspections and continues transit with full crew, full colonist complement, and standard consumables loadout for an extended onward journey.

The Authority notes, for the curious, that a colony charter arriving already this far into an unlogged transit, sealed at both ends, is, in the Authority's considerable experience, quite rare.

*[hand unknown — registry stamp legible, vessel name redacted at time of filing]*

> **Corvid:** "inbound from beyond the cluster's charted edge" is as much as this document is willing to say, and it is maddeningly unspecific — it could mean she came from three systems over or three hundred. I believe, though I stress this only as belief, that this concerns the same vessel discussed elsewhere in this volume: the ship later remembered as the *Dusk's Long Shadow*. I will not pretend to more certainty than that.

## From the Personal Diary of a Dockworker at a Waystation Along the Route (likely [[avire|Avire]])

Loaded the last of the crates myself, on account of half my crew calling in sick, which I now suspect was less illness and more good sense on their part, though I couldn't tell you why I suspect that. Strange ship. Ordinary enough to look at — colony hull, standard rotation drum, nothing you wouldn't see on a dozen other charters — but the loading manifest didn't match what actually went into the hold, not by my count, and the foreman told me twice not to worry about the discrepancy, which is exactly the number of times it takes me to start worrying about something.

Talked to one of her crew while we worked, briefly, before an officer called him off. Asked him, casual as I could manage, how long they'd been underway. He said "longer than you'd believe," and something in the way he said it made me not want to press further. Didn't get a straight answer on where she'd started from. Got the distinct impression he didn't much want to think about it either.

Watched her burn out-system that evening, still heading further out, not toward any of the settled approaches I recognized.

> **Corvid:** I have chosen, deliberately, not to speculate in this entry about what the discrepancy in the manifest might have concealed. What interests me more, on this reading, is "longer than you'd believe" — a crewman's own words, suggesting even the people aboard understood their journey as unusually long, from somewhere unusually far. That is not nothing. It is also not an answer. Readers looking for the disturbance in [[the-song-beneath-the-stars|the substrate itself]] around this same period will find nothing in this diary to confirm or deny it — this dockworker had more immediate worries than the Song.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've talked to old Guild hands who'll tell you, with total confidence, that she came coreward — out past Rado, out past anything on a common chart, somewhere in toward the old worlds proper. I've talked to others who'll tell you, with equal confidence, that "coreward" is just what people say when they mean "I don't actually know and don't want to admit it." I lean toward believing she came from a long way off. I don't lean toward believing anyone who claims to know exactly how far.

## Three Competing Accounts of Her Origin, Gathered from Later Oral Tradition

**The first account**, told among [[perope|Perope]] cluster escort pilots, holds that she departed from somewhere deep coreward — a world or shipyard near the old heart of things, long before anyone in this cluster ever laid eyes on her, and that her transit through Perope and Tabet space was only the last, smallest leg of a much longer journey whose true starting point nobody here ever charted.

**The second account**, told on [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Hypsila]] among guildhouse traders, holds that she was never coreward at all, but built and launched from somewhere much closer — a quiet, well-funded operation the traders can never quite name, and that the "coreward" story is a deliberate misdirection, planted by whoever paid for her passage to keep the curious looking in the wrong direction entirely.

**The third account**, the rarest of the three and the one Corvid trusts least and repeats anyway, holds that she had no single point of departure at all — that she had been underway, in one form or another, for so long before anyone in this cluster saw her that the question "where did she come from" stopped having a meaningful answer generations before her crew ever hailed a Perope dockworker.

*[all three: hand unknown, gathered secondhand, no two tellings fully agreeing even within each account]*

> **Corvid:** I print all three because I believe all three, in the sense that I believe people told them sincerely, and I believe none of them, in the sense that none can be verified. I lean toward the first account myself — "deep coreward" — mostly because it is the version told by the people who spent the most professional time actually watching ships arrive and depart in this cluster, and who had the least motive to lie about it. But I want to be honest: that is a guess, dressed up as a preference, and nothing more.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I lean toward the second account, for exactly the opposite reason Corvid gives the first one credit. Traders lie for profit, sure, but guildhouse traders also notice when money moves quietly through a system, and quiet money is exactly what you'd expect behind a ship somebody wanted people to believe came from somewhere else. I don't say I'm right. I say Corvid and I have been arguing about this specific point for longer than either of us wants to admit in print.
>
> **Cinder:** I believe the second account, and not because it's the most interesting one — because it's the most familiar. Quiet money, an unlogged launch, a "commercial sensitivity" seal slapped over the whole business before anyone downstream could ask a real question. I've watched concerns and consortiums pull exactly this trick on a dozen worlds. Claiming she came from somewhere impossibly far is always cheaper than admitting whose backyard she actually left from, and who signed off on letting her go without a proper accounting.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Not everything's a corporate cover-up, Cinder.
>
> **Cinder:** Show me the one that wasn't, and I'll reconsider.
>
> **Corvid:** For once I don't entirely dismiss him, which unsettles me more than the ship does.

## From a Letter, Author and Recipient Both Unrecorded

They tell me the posting is an honor, and perhaps it is, but I confess I do not entirely understand what it is I have volunteered for, only that the pay is extraordinary and the confidentiality clause longer than any I have signed before. I am told the work is important — vital, even, to "solving a problem that has troubled us for generations" — and that I should be proud to be chosen for it.

I am proud. I am also, if I am honest with this page and no one else, a little afraid, though I could not tell you of what, precisely. Perhaps that is simply what important work feels like, from the inside, before you understand it well enough to know if the fear is warranted.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I have wondered, more than once, whether this letter was written by someone who boarded that ship, wherever she began her journey. I have no way to confirm it, and I have decided, on reflection, that I would rather not know for certain. Some fears, I think, are more honestly left unanswered.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Agreed. Some doors you don't knock on even in a book nobody living can be blamed for.
