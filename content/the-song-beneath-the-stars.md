## From a Devotional Pamphlet, Sect and World Unrecorded

The Song runs beneath all things, brothers and sisters, and has always run there, from before any of us were born to now, and it will run there after we are gone from this life into whatever waits beyond it. The engines of our ships ride the Song to carry us between the stars faster than light itself allows. The gifted among us hear the Song directly, without engine or instrument, and it is through their hearing that we speak across distances no voice alone could cross. We are, all of us, a little closer to that Song than our ancestors on the homeworld ever knew — some of us close enough to sing back.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I have gathered a great many pre-Scream fragments describing what they variously call the Song, the Deep Hum, the Metadimensional Current, or simply "the gift" — all of them, near as I can tell, groping toward some understanding of the same underlying phenomenon: a substrate beneath the visible universe that both carried our ships between stars and, in a fraction of our people, manifested as psychic ability. What none of them knew — what none of them could have known — is that the Song was capable of screaming. They wrote of it the way you might write of the tide: reliable, ancient, faintly holy. I do not think a single author in this volume expected it to ever do anything but hold steady.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've met exactly one person gifted enough to hear anything like this "Song" firsthand, in the present day. I asked her once what it sounded like now, compared to what the old pamphlets describe. She didn't answer me for a long moment, and then she said "it doesn't sound like anything holy anymore," and changed the subject. I've never asked her again.

## From a Physician's Case Notes, Practice Location Unrecorded

Third patient this month presenting with the same complaint: persistent, low-grade auditory disturbance, described variously as a hum, a hiss, or "a sound like a held breath," accompanied in two of the three cases by mild but genuine precognitive flashes lasting no more than a few seconds. All three patients are registered gifted individuals. None report the symptom worsening beyond mild discomfort. I have recommended rest and reduced exposure to concentrated crowds, per standard practice guidance, though I confess the guidance feels inadequate to what these patients describe.

A colleague in a neighboring district reports an uptick in similar cases. I have written to ask whether this is a regional pattern or something wider. I have not yet received a reply.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I include this clinical note with a heaviness I have not felt writing almost anything else in this book. I do not know how close to the Scream itself this physician was writing — weeks, months, longer — but the pattern described here, gifted individuals across multiple districts independently reporting the same faint, worsening disturbance in the substrate beneath everything, reads to me now as an early tremor of the catastrophe to come. I do not think this physician ever received the reply they were waiting for.
>
> **Cinder:** "I have not yet received a reply." Of course not. Some review office somewhere had that physician's letter, filed it under insufficient data, and went back to whatever mattered more that afternoon. Every warning in this book that could have counted got buried by somebody whose whole job was deciding it wasn't worth acting on yet. That's not one unlucky coincidence. That's how every authority in this cluster has always handled the people beneath it.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Can't argue with that one either. Feels bad agreeing with you twice in one book, Cinder.
>
> **Corvid:** Let it stand. He's not wrong, for once.

## From an Engineer's Technical Log, Vessel and Route Unrecorded

Logged an anomalous reading on the Song-drive resonance array again today — third time this route, always at the same relative position, always gone by the time I can get a second instrument on it. Chief engineer says it's instrument drift and not worth a maintenance flag. I've flagged it anyway. Call it professional stubbornness. If I'm wrong, we've lost ten minutes to an overcautious junior engineer. If I'm right and nobody flagged it, we've lost something considerably harder to get back.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I do not know if this engineer's instinct was ever vindicated, or ignored, or simply overtaken by events too large for any single flagged reading to have prevented. I include it as the last entry in this section because it is, of everything gathered here, the closest any pre-Scream author came to actually noticing something was wrong before it was far too late for noticing to matter.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I'd like to think somebody, somewhere, eventually read that flag and took it seriously. I have no evidence for that. I'm choosing to believe it anyway. Call it professional stubbornness of my own.
>
> **Cinder:** Miners in [[the-porth-belt|the Porth belt]] were handling something with the same faint resonance long before anyone put a name to it either, near as Corvid and I can tell. Whatever this substrate actually was, ordinary people kept brushing up against it for generations before it screamed. Nobody with the authority to investigate properly ever did.
