# On the Song Beneath the Stars

What the old fragments say about psychic gift and starflight, in the last years before both changed forever.

## Devotional Pamphlet, Sect and World Unrecorded

The Song runs beneath all things, brothers and sisters, and has always run there, from before any of us were born to now, and it will run there after we are gone from this life into whatever waits beyond it. The engines of our ships ride the Song to carry us between the stars faster than light itself allows. The gifted among us hear the Song directly, without engine or instrument, and it is through their hearing that we speak across distances no voice alone could cross. We are, all of us, a little closer to that Song than our ancestors on the homeworld ever knew — some of us close enough to sing back.

*[hand unknown, undated]*

> **[FIELD NOTE — M. Okonkwo-Reyes, Archive Adept, 2nd Rank, 3176.094]**
> Fragments describing what they variously call the Song, the Deep Hum, the Metadimensional Current, or simply "the gift" all seem to be groping toward some understanding of the same underlying phenomenon: a substrate beneath the visible universe that both carried ships between stars and, in a fraction of the population, manifested as psychic ability. What none of these authors knew — what none of them could have known — is that the Song was capable of screaming. They wrote of it the way you might write of the tide: reliable, ancient, faintly holy.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Sella Voth, Guild of Pilots escort captain, 3151.294]**
> I've met exactly one person gifted enough to hear anything like this "Song" firsthand, in the present day. Asked her once what it sounded like now, compared to what the old pamphlets describe. She didn't answer for a long moment, then said "it doesn't sound like anything holy anymore," and changed the subject. Never asked her again.

## Dispatch — Physician's Case Notes, Practice Location Unrecorded

```
FIELD DISPATCH (RECOVERED, PRE-SCREAM)
Transmission: recovered, addressee unknown
```

Third patient this month presenting with the same complaint: persistent, low-grade auditory disturbance, described variously as a hum, a hiss, or "a sound like a held breath," accompanied in two of the three cases by mild but genuine precognitive flashes lasting no more than a few seconds. All three patients are registered gifted individuals. None report the symptom worsening beyond mild discomfort. I have recommended rest and reduced exposure to concentrated crowds, per standard practice guidance, though I confess the guidance feels inadequate to what these patients describe.

A colleague in a neighboring district reports an uptick in similar cases. I have written to ask whether this is a regional pattern or something wider. I have not yet received a reply.

*— unsigned physician, undated*

> **[FIELD NOTE — Cor. Dassin, Cerberus Corps field agent, 3188.173]**
> "I have not yet received a reply." Of course not. Some review office somewhere had that letter, filed it under insufficient data, and moved on. Every warning in this book that could have counted got buried by somebody whose whole job was deciding it wasn't worth acting on yet. That's not one unlucky coincidence. That's how every authority in this cluster has always handled the people beneath it.

## Dispatch — Engineer's Technical Log, Vessel and Route Unrecorded

```
FIELD DISPATCH (RECOVERED, PRE-SCREAM)
Transmission: recovered
```

Logged an anomalous reading on the Song-drive resonance array again today — third time this route, always at the same relative position, always gone by the time I can get a second instrument on it. Chief engineer says it's instrument drift and not worth a maintenance flag. I've flagged it anyway. Call it professional stubbornness. If I'm wrong, we've lost ten minutes to an overcautious junior engineer. If I'm right and nobody flagged it, we've lost something considerably harder to get back.

*— unsigned engineer, undated*

> **[FIELD NOTE — Tamsin Ilarra, salvage contractor, 3199.010]**
> Miners in [[the-porth-belt|the Porth belt]] were handling something with the same faint resonance long before anyone put a name to it either. Whatever this substrate actually was, ordinary people kept brushing up against it for generations before it screamed. I'd like to think somebody, somewhere, eventually read this engineer's flag and took it seriously. I have no evidence for that. I'm choosing to believe it anyway.
