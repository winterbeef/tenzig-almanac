## From a Trade Assessment Filed by the Aquilanes Mercantile Bureau

Eurymem (0206) and Hypsila (0406) present, in the Bureau's assessment, the two most aggressively growing economies in this quadrant, and the two most aggressively competitive. Both worlds have petitioned this cycle for expanded naval contracts; both have accused the other, in formal diplomatic channels, of industrial espionage; both are, in the Bureau's professional opinion, almost certainly correct.

Eurymem's growth is underwritten substantially by a single large concern — the Eurymedon Concern, a shipbuilding and heavy-engineering house with unusually deep government contacts for a company of its size and youth. Hypsila's growth is more diffuse, spread across a dozen mid-sized guildhouses, none dominant, all suspicious of each other nearly as much as they are suspicious of Eurymem.

> **Corvid:** the rivalry this assessor describes did not end with the Scream. It somehow got worse. Six hundred years later, Eurymem and Hypsila are still trading in spycraft and diplomatic sabotage as a matter of course, and I have come to suspect that whatever changed about the galaxy in the intervening centuries, old grudges between neighbors were not on the list.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've smuggled for both sides of that rivalry at different points in my career, sometimes in the same season. Neither of them has ever once asked me to feel bad about it. I respect the consistency, honestly.

## From an Internal Memorandum of the Eurymedon Concern, Marked "Restricted Circulation"

The Board wishes to remind all division heads that the Concern's current slate of government-contracted work is not, and should not be characterized to outside parties as, a matter of public record. Division heads fielding inquiries regarding "the [[perope|Perope]] contract" or "the resupply project" should refer such inquiries to Legal without comment.

The Board further wishes to remind division heads that the Concern's continued preferential access to Mandate-adjacent funding channels depends substantially on the discretion of everyone in this room, and that discretion, unlike most things this quarter, is not a line item the Board is prepared to see cut.

*[hand unknown — Concern letterhead, water-damaged]*

> **Corvid:** I do not know precisely what "the Perope contract" or "the resupply project" refers to, and I have chosen — deliberately, and with some discomfort — not to speculate further in these pages. See [[the-unmarked-departure|the entry on the ship that passed through Perope unlogged]] for what little I am willing to say. What I will say here is only this: the Eurymedon Concern, as a named company, does not appear in any record from the last five centuries. Whatever it was building toward, whatever contract it was so careful to keep quiet, it did not survive under its own name to see how the story ended. Something did grow from what it left behind. I have looked into what that something is, and what I found is not mine to publish here.
>
> **Two-Coin:** For once, Corvid and I agree on something: leave it alone. I have poked at fewer things in my career than I'd like to admit, and every single one I regretted poking at looked exactly like this memo before I poked.
>
> **Cinder:** Cowards, both of you. "Discretion, unlike most things this quarter, is not a line item we're prepared to see cut" is a company admitting, in writing, that it's being paid to keep something quiet from the same public whose taxes fund its Mandate-adjacent contracts. That's not a boring footnote to skip past. That's the whole crime, dressed up in accounting language, and neither of you will say so plainly.
>
> **Corvid:** Some of us would like to survive the reading of this book, Cinder.

## From a Hypsila Guildhouse Broadsheet, Distributed Dockside

CITIZENS OF HYPSILA: the Eurymem Trade Delegation which departed our docks last cycle did NOT, as they claim, come here merely "to observe local shipbuilding techniques." Our own harbor-masters report their survey instruments were pointed rather more often at OUR guild ledgers than at any hull in dry dock.

We do not say this to inflame sentiment. We say this because it is TRUE, and because a rival who will lie about a harbor tour will lie about a trade agreement, and we would rather our fellow citizens negotiate with open eyes than closed ones.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Two-Coin:** I have nothing to add to this one. Some things really do speak for themselves, across any number of centuries.
>
> **Corvid:** Agreed, for what that's worth coming from me.
