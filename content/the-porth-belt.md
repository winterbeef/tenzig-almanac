## From the Ledger of Factor Beniamin Curro, Independent Grain Trader

Diverted through the Porth belt on a supply run and found it a rougher sort of prosperity than the core worlds — hard mining crews, harder bar fights, and a mineral trade in something the local assayers call cherambilth, which fetches a price I found frankly baffling until I saw a sample myself. It has a faint warmth to it that has nothing to do with any radioactivity my instruments could detect, and the miners speak of it with a reverence I have otherwise only heard directed at things people pray to.

I bought a small piece for curiosity's sake. I have caught myself, more than once since, simply holding it while thinking of nothing in particular, and finding the thinking of nothing strangely restful. I do not entirely understand my own behavior around this rock, and I do not entirely trust it either.

> **Corvid:** cherambilth's peculiar properties — its faint resonance with living minds, well documented in the present era — were evidently known and traded on, if imperfectly understood, six centuries before anyone gave the phenomenon a proper name. See [[the-song-beneath-the-stars|the entry on the Song]] for the wider pattern this seems to belong to. I find it oddly comforting that some mysteries are allowed to remain mysteries across such a very long span of time.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I carry a piece myself. Don't ask what I paid for it. Corvid's right that it's restful, and I'd add: that's exactly why I don't fully trust it either. Nothing that makes you feel that good is only doing one thing to you.

## From a Settlement Charter, Okotoks Mining Concern

The undersigned settlers, having secured mineral rights to the Okotoks claim under standard Mandate registry, do hereby establish a self-governing mining collective, to be administered by elected overseer and council, with all extracted value divided per the attached share agreement. The Concern further commits to maintaining full and open records of all extraction activity, available to any registered claim-holder upon request, in perpetuity.

*[hand unknown — Okotoks settlement archive]*

> **Corvid:** "in perpetuity" is a phrase I have learned, reading enough of these old documents, to regard with a certain rueful affection. Whether Okotoks kept this promise for the two, three, four centuries it might have managed before the Scream rewrote every promise anyone had made, I cannot say. I can say only that Okotoks, in some form, still stands, still mines, and is still led by people who take the old charter seriously enough to invoke it.
>
> **Two-Coin:** They do still invoke it. I've watched a claim dispute get settled by someone literally quoting this exact charter, word for word, at a council meeting. Made my hair stand up a little, honestly.

## From the Log of a Salvage Captain Working the Belt's Outer Debris Field

Strange wreck out past the third claim marker today — not naval, not civilian, not anything my registry database recognizes at all. Hull geometry all wrong for anyone I know how to build for. Crew wanted to go aboard and strip it for parts. I said no, and I could not entirely explain why beyond a feeling in my gut that some doors are better left shut than opened, whatever's behind them might be worth.

We logged the coordinates and moved on. I have thought about that wreck more than any of the ordinary ones I've actually salvaged.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I will not speculate here about the origin of a wreck "wrong for anyone I know how to build for," found drifting in a debris field that the present era also associates with ruins and relics considerably older than anything human hands raised. Compare [[edino-and-the-silent-people|the survey fragment describing a masterless vessel]] elsewhere in this volume — I do not say the two are related, only that they rhyme. I will say only that this captain's instinct not to go aboard strikes me, six hundred years later, as very possibly the wisest decision recorded anywhere in this entire volume.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Agreed without reservation. I have gone aboard exactly one wreck I had a bad feeling about, once, when I was younger and needed the money more than I needed my instincts intact. I still won't talk about what we found. Learn from the captain, not from me.
>
> **Cinder:** That's the story that actually matters, Two-Coin, more than another wreck in a debris field. Not what you found. Who'd have profited off it if you'd brought it back. That's always the real question, and it's the one nobody in authority ever wants asked out loud.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Maybe. I'm still not telling it. Drop it.
