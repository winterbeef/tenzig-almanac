## Overheard and Recorded by a Later Hand, Tavern Unnamed

They say there's a corner of [[the-porth-belt|the belt out past Porth]] where ships go quiet and don't come back on schedule, not lost exactly, just late in a way nobody can explain after the fact. They say the crews that do come back don't talk about it, and the ones who talk too freely about it don't get hired again.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I have gathered three separate versions of this exact rumor from three unrelated sources, which in my experience means either it happened once and got retold badly, or it happened more than once and nobody wanted to say so plainly. I do not know which.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I know a crew that came back late from that stretch once. I bought the pilot a drink and asked her about it, direct, no games. She looked at me for a long time and then asked if we could talk about literally anything else. We did. I never asked again.

## From a Warlord's Personal Correspondence, Recipient a Rival, Both Unnamed

You call what I hold a "petty fiefdom" and I will grant you the pettiness is a matter of scale, not of ambition. I hold three worlds you do not, feed people you have let starve twice this decade, and sleep considerably better than a man who needs three separate food-tasters to trust his own table. Keep your insults. I will keep my worlds.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** neither party to this correspondence appears by name in any other document I have gathered. I include it purely because it is a wonderful piece of writing, and because it is a useful reminder that not every power in this cluster's history left a large enough mark to be remembered by anything but its own bragging.
>
> **Two-Coin:** For once, no notes. This one's perfect as it is.
>
> **Cinder:** Two men, each one certain his throne is the legitimate one because it happens to be smaller. I've got no more patience for a "petty fiefdom" lord than I do for a Concern boardroom. Same disease, different scale, same excuse for why the people under them have to go without.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Not every argument needs a manifesto attached, Cinder.

## A Children's Rhyme, Collected From Oral Tradition, World Unrecorded

*Star to star and hull to hull,*
*mind the Song when the night is full,*
*close your eyes and count to nine,*
*the ones who hear it don't need eyes.*

*[hand unknown — transcribed by a folklorist, original context lost]*

> **Corvid:** I do not know how old this rhyme is, nor whether it predates the years just before the Scream by generations or was composed fresh in that same anxious decade, half in earnest and half as a child's game to make an unsettling subject bearable. See [[the-song-beneath-the-stars|the entry on the Song]] for the more sober version of the same fear. I include it because it is, of everything in this volume, the fragment I have found myself unable to stop quietly repeating to myself, late at night, for reasons I have not fully examined.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I taught this rhyme to a dockworker's kid once without thinking about where I'd learned it. Her mother was not pleased with me. I don't blame her, in hindsight.

## From a Merchant's Marginal Note, Scrawled in the Corner of an Otherwise Unremarkable Cargo Manifest

Strange feeling in the market today. Can't name it. Everyone a little too cheerful, like we all know something's coming and have collectively decided not to mention it. Sold everything at a fair price and left before dark anyway. Didn't used to be the sort of trader who left before dark.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** this note bears no date, no world, and no name, and I cannot prove it was written anywhere near the Scream at all. I have chosen, for reasons that will not surprise anyone who has read this far, to place it last in this volume regardless. Some fragments do not need a date to tell you exactly when they were written. I think this is one of them.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Whoever this was, they were right to leave before dark. I've learned to trust that exact feeling myself, more than once, and I've never once regretted listening to it.
>
> **Cinder:** That's the last of what I've got the stomach to add tonight. Six hundred years on, I still don't know what any of it amounts to — but somebody was still writing "I have not yet received a reply" (see [[the-song-beneath-the-stars]]) right up until the day it stopped mattering, and somebody has to keep writing these things down so the next poor bastard gets some kind of warning. That's the only faith I've got left in any of this. It'll have to do.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Didn't expect that out of you.
>
> **Cinder:** Don't get used to it.
