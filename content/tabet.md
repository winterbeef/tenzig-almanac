## From the Tourist Pamphlet of the Tabet Cultural Board (Third Printing)

Welcome, traveler, to Tabet — a world proud to call itself the beating heart of the [[perope|Perope]] cluster! Whatever business brings you through our docks, we invite you to stay an extra night for the Lantern Processional, held every third evening in the Old Quarter, where six hundred years of continuous settlement have produced a culture unmatched anywhere in this stretch of the map. (The Cultural Board would like to note that [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Hypsila]]'s tourism office makes an identical claim about their own Processional, and that this claim is, as with most things Hypsila says, simply untrue.)

> **Corvid:** I have looked, and I cannot find anything resembling a "Cultural Board" on Tabet in any record less than five centuries old. Either it did not survive, or it was quietly folded into something else that preferred not to keep the old name. Tabet itself, I am glad to report, very much survived — it remains, by every account I can gather, the single most crowded and culturally alive world in the region. Some things, it seems, the Scream could not touch.
>
> **Cinder:** "Cultural Board" is doing a lot of quiet work in that pamphlet title. Any world can throw a lantern parade. It takes a government office to decide a culture needs a board to certify it, usually right around the time somebody figures out how to sell tickets to it.
>
> **Two-Coin:** You say that like Tabet needed the certifying. It didn't. It's been the liveliest port in this cluster longer than any board's been around to take credit for it.

## From a Shipping Manifest, Tabet Customs House

Declared cargo: agricultural seed stock (14 crates), replacement parts for atmospheric processors (6 crates), "personal effects" (2 crates, undeclared weight, customs fee paid in full without dispute).

*[hand unknown — customs stamp partially legible]*

> **Corvid:** I include this dull little manifest only because "personal effects, undeclared weight, fee paid without dispute" is, in my long experience reading old customs paper, one of the most reliable phrases in the language for "please do not look inside these crates." What was actually inside, six hundred years gone, is a question no customs house will ever answer now.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I have moved cargo under exactly that description more than once, some of it through [[avire|Avire]] on the same run. I decline to say what was in mine, either. Some traditions really do carry across the centuries.
