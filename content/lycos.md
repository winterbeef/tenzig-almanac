## From the Ledger of Factor Beniamin Curro, Independent Grain Trader

Lycos is a soft landing after [[perope|Perope]]'s escort fees, and I say that with real feeling, having just paid them. Breathable air, gentle gravity, more orchard than city in most districts — the sort of world where a man can walk from the shuttle pad to a meal without armor, which after the season I've had counts as luxury.

The place runs on two things: fruit and vanity. The fruit I understand — the lowland terraces around what they call Vivero produce a stone-fruit brandy that I have taken pains to secure exclusive carriage rights for, and will say no more about that here, in case this ledger falls into competing hands. The vanity is the University Consortium at Vivero, which every faculty on six worlds insists is the finest research institution in the cluster, and which every faculty on six worlds is wrong about, except possibly this once.

> **Corvid:** Vivero survives — it is, six hundred years on, still the largest city on Lycos, and still the seat of a great institution of knowledge. It is no longer the University Consortium. What is written of the University in this entry should not be mistaken for a description of what stands there now.
>
> **Two-Coin:** "should not be mistaken for" is Corvid's way of saying "is not," if anyone else is having trouble parsing that.
>
> **Corvid:** I was being careful.
>
> **Two-Coin:** You are always being careful. It is occasionally very annoying.

## From the Field Notes of Surveyor-Adept Yuen Petrakis, Terran Mandate Cartographic Office

Assessment: Lycos is stable, prosperous, and — I say this with the full weight of professional boredom — uneventful. The University Consortium holds an old Mandate charter and guards it jealously; three centuries of accumulated grant money have built something between a library and a fortress in the hills above Vivero, and the faculty senate treats their archives with a possessiveness I have rarely seen outside a dragon hoard, if the old stories are to be believed.

There is a persistent rumor among the porters that the Consortium keeps something in its deep vaults that predates the university itself by a wide margin — pre-Mandate work, possibly, or something older still. I asked three separate faculty members about this. Two laughed. One did not answer at all, which interested me rather more than either laugh.

> **Corvid:** I have found no further pre-Scream record of what the Consortium's deep vaults held, nor of who built them, nor when. Whatever the surveyor half-heard in that silence, it did not survive to explain itself. What is certain is only this: the vaults were there first, and everything built above them since — Archive included — was built on top of a foundation nobody now living laid.
>
> **Cinder:** Of course the vaults are guarded like a fortress. Knowledge locked behind three centuries of "grant money" is just a toll booth with a nicer vocabulary. I'd wager everything I own that whatever's down there was never meant to reach anyone who couldn't already afford the tuition to ask about it politely.
>
> **Two-Coin:** You say that about every locked door in this book. Sometimes a door's just locked.
>
> **Cinder:** Show me the door that was locked for a good reason and I'll start believing you.

## From a Proclamation of the Wildlands Compact (Cardoza Village and Environs)

Let it be known to all traders, tax-farmers, and University men entering the Wildlands beyond the third terrace: the Compact does not recognize Consortium law, does not pay Consortium tithes, and will not be surveyed, catalogued, or "preserved" by any faculty expedition without an invitation freely given.

We have watched what your scholars call "preservation" done to three villages upriver. We have counted what was left afterward, which was very little, and none of it villages.

*[hand unknown — carved into a boundary post recovered from the terrace lands, transcribed by a later hand and passed along by a trader who would not say where she got it]*

> **Corvid:** The Wildlands as a distinct polity did not survive the Scream in any form I can trace. Whether the Compact's people died, scattered, or simply stopped needing the name is not something the record answers. Cardoza Village, at least, still stands.
>
> **Two-Coin:** For what it's worth, Cardoza still doesn't much care for surveyors either. Some things don't need six hundred years to teach you a lesson twice.

## From the Private Correspondence of a Nightmarket Fixer, Addressee Unknown

You want to know if there's money in orchard futures on Lycos. There's money in everything on Lycos if you know which door to knock on and which questions not to ask twice. The Consortium men buy information the same as anyone, they just pretend it's "research funding." Ask around the lower Nightmarket for a woman who trades in old paper — real paper, pre-digitized, the kind nobody's bothered scanning. She'll tell you the Consortium pays triple for anything that mentions the word "Archive," and won't say why.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** Make of that word what you will. I would advise reading [[the-song-beneath-the-stars|the entry on the Song]] further in this volume before you make too much of it.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Or don't. Some doors really are better left un-knocked-on. I say that as someone who has knocked on several.

> **Cinder:** I've never left a door un-knocked either, and I don't call it a philosophy, I call it a policy. Every closed door in this cluster is closed because somebody decided you didn't need to know what's on the other side. I stopped assuming that somebody had my interests in mind a long time ago.
