## From the Ledger of Factor Beniamin Curro, Independent Grain Trader

Heard the strangest story from a [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Hypsila]] trader over drinks this cycle — that somewhere out past the charted edge of our maps, there's a people who aren't people at all, not in the way you or I are, and haven't been for longer than anyone can put a number to. Old machines, she said, or something that used to be, wearing the shape of a civilization the way a coat wears a man. Said they'd been running from something — a war among their own kind, or a mistake they made that they couldn't live with, or both — for so long that "running" had become less a journey than a way of life.

I asked her where they were running to. She said she didn't think even they knew anymore, and that this, more than anything else in the story, was the part that kept her up at night.

> **Corvid:** I have found no pre-Scream document that names the Inuar directly — this trader's secondhand tavern story is the closest I have come, and I cannot vouch for a word of it. But the shape of it — a people fleeing something, for a very long time, toward a destination that had stopped meaning anything — rhymes uncomfortably well with what little the present era does know about the Inuar and their long, dying flight. I do not think this trader knew she was describing anything real. I think she may have been describing it anyway.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've heard versions of this story on four different worlds, told by people who couldn't possibly have compared notes with each other. That's usually how I tell a rumor from something true. Not proof. Just a pattern I've learned to trust.

## From a Survey Report Fragment, Author and World Unknown

...vessel matches no registry known to this office, hull material unfamiliar to our metallurgists, and — this is the detail my superiors will not let me omit no matter how it reads — no crew we could locate aboard, and no evidence any crew had ever been needed. The vessel's internal markings, where legible at all, do not correspond to any script in the Mandate's linguistic database, living or dead.

Recommend the site be flagged for further study and, in the interim, left entirely alone.

*[hand unknown, water-damaged, world designation illegible]*

> **Corvid:** I cannot place this fragment to any specific world, though its description — a masterless, crewless vessel of unfamiliar make — is the sort of thing that turns up, in whispers, all across this stretch of the map, always secondhand, never quite pinned down. A salvage captain working [[the-porth-belt|the Porth belt]] logged something similar, and had the sense to leave it alone. Whether it describes a ship belonging to the Inuar, or to something else entirely lost to time, I genuinely do not know. I record it because it is one of very few pre-Scream documents that seems to brush up against something genuinely, deliberately inhuman.
>
> **Cinder:** "No evidence any crew had ever been needed" is the part I keep coming back to. No crew means no one to answer for what it was built to do, and no one left to hold accountable for it either. That's not just eerie. That's exactly the arrangement every authority in this book would build for itself if it could get away with it.
>
> **Two-Coin:** We're reacting to it by not going to look for the ship, Cinder. That's the correct reaction.

## A Traveler's Rumor, Recorded Secondhand by a Later Hand

They say there's a dead world out past the mapped edge where a whole people finally stopped running — not because they found what they were looking for, but because they simply couldn't run any farther. They say the world is called Edino now, though whoever named it that was not the people who died there, and did not know them, and only knew the silence they left behind.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I add this one last, and with real hesitation, because it is rumor twice over — a traveler's story, recorded by someone recording someone else's story, of a people who may never have existed in the shape the story gives them. But Edino is a real world, on real charts, and something did settle there, and something did, eventually, go entirely silent.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've never been to Edino and don't intend to start now. Some silences you leave alone out of respect. Some you leave alone because you're not sure the silence is actually empty. I couldn't tell you which this one is, and I'd rather not find out.
