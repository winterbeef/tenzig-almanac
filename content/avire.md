## Field Notes

> **[FIELD NOTE — commissioning officer, TMN Anemoi's Gift, 2598.003]**
> *Anemoi's Gift* enters active naval service this cycle, assigned to the [[perope|Perope]] corridor as a forward resupply and repair platform for escort and survey vessels operating the [[tabet|Tabet]] approach. She carries a crew of one hundred forty, full fabrication and repair bays, and enough reaction mass to keep three escort squadrons fueled through an extended deployment.
>
> Her captain, on commissioning, is recorded only as remarking that she is "an ugly ship for an ugly job, and I mean that as the highest compliment I know how to give a vessel."

> **[FIELD NOTE — junior officer, TMN Anemoi's Gift, name redacted by a later hand, 2599.140]**
> Third week on the Perope corridor and I am beginning to understand why the old hands call this posting "the ugly job" without a trace of irony. We refuel, rearm, and repair anything that limps in from the corridor, ask no questions we're not ordered to ask, and watch the survey traffic go by in both directions like a slow parade of people who all seem to know something we don't.
>
> Overheard two escort pilots talking dockside about "the second rock work" in low voices, the way you talk about something you're being paid well not to talk about at all. Didn't ask. Wrote it down instead, because some habits from the Academy never leave you.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Nine-Finger Sable, smuggler (self-described), 3140.301]**
> Drank in that bar for a solid decade of my life before somebody told me it used to be a warship's actual wardroom. Explains a lot about the acoustics, honestly. Explains nothing about the drinks, which have always been terrible.

> **[FIELD NOTE — M. Okonkwo-Reyes, Archive Adept, 2nd Rank, 3176.088]**
> For the record, since nobody else here bothered: the description of Avire station that circulates today — a converted Ticonderoga-class resupply hull, holding at that Lagrange point for going on thirty years under its current keeper — matches the old commissioning record closely enough that I don't believe it's coincidence. If I'm right, the bar they call the Wardroom is sitting more or less exactly where the actual wardroom used to be. Make of that what you will. I find it strangely moving and I am not a sentimental woman.

## Traveler's Notes: Avire, Present Day

*Unofficial addendum, kept informally by whoever's tending bar this decade and updated by word of mouth — treat as current, not gospel.*

**Where to drink.** The Wardroom remains the social center of the station and the recommended stop — bad drinks, decent company, and a bartender who keeps order without fuss. Further round the ring, a louder, cheaper room going by "Drift Nine" is best avoided after second shift; the fights that start there don't always stay contained, and station security's response time on that stretch of the ring is slow by design — the keeper doesn't want to pay for a permanent post out there. A third, unnamed room tucked near the old fabrication bay serves no food, plays no music, and is where you go if you actually need to hear yourself think.

**Entertainment and company.** A modest gaming floor runs near the docking ring on a rotating schedule; the games are straight, or straight enough that nobody's proven otherwise in living memory. Company can be arranged properly through the station's hospitality office — licensed, unglamorous, safe. Informal arrangements exist too, as they do everywhere a lot of transient crew pass through, and carry the informal risks that come with skipping the office. Information brokers work the Wardroom's back tables most evenings; for a price they'll tell you who's hiring, who's shipping what, and who to avoid. Reliability varies considerably by broker, and nobody will vouch for one in front of another.

**The cost of looking the other way.** None of the following is posted anywhere, and all of it is common knowledge to anyone who's passed through twice: an expedited inspection to skip the cargo queue runs roughly 40–80 credits, station-keeper's discretion. A manifest the inspector would rather not itemize too closely runs considerably more, rarely under 200, and is not something to ask about twice if refused once. An extra berth-day without the paperwork to match costs about 200 credits, paid to the dockmaster directly and never to the office.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Whit, courier, 3199.180]**
> Prices above are what I've paid personally, and I move a lot of cargo through here. Don't try to talk the dockmaster down — she doesn't negotiate, she just quotes you the number again, slower, until you pay it.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Nine-Finger Sable, smuggler (self-described), 3199.181]**
> Can confirm the gaming floor's straight. Can also confirm I've lost enough credits there to have checked very thoroughly.
