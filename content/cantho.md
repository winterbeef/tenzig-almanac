## From the Ledger of Factor Beniamin Curro, Independent Grain Trader

I will say this for Cantho: I have never in my career had a contract dispute resolved so quickly, so fairly, or so completely to my satisfaction. The Central Planning Commission's arbitration office settled a six-month disagreement with a [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Hypsila]] supplier inside of two hours, cited precedent I'd never heard of, and produced a settlement both parties found genuinely reasonable. I have never seen anything like it before or since.

I do not entirely trust it, and I could not tell you why, and that bothers me more than any dishonest dealing ever has.

> **Corvid:** Factor Curro was not alone in this feeling, if the rest of the surviving record is any guide. Cantho, both then and now by every account I can gather, runs with a smoothness that traders across six centuries have independently found more disquieting than reassuring. I do not know what runs Cantho. I am not sure anyone outside Cantho ever has.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I turned down a delivery contract there once for no better reason than I didn't like how fast the harbor office answered my message. Two minutes. Nobody answers a stranger in two minutes unless they already knew you were coming.
>
> **Cinder:** "Efficient" is the word every good government uses for itself right before somebody finds out what the efficiency actually cost, and who paid it. I've never once met a commission that ran that smooth without somebody underneath the smoothness absorbing the difference.

## From the Field Notes of Surveyor-Adept Yuen Petrakis, Terran Mandate Cartographic Office

Assessment: Cantho's Central Planning Commission governs with an efficiency that borders on the uncanny — infrastructure maintained ahead of schedule, resource allocation with no waste I could detect in three weeks of auditing, public disputes resolved before, in one case, the dispute had been formally filed. I requested an audience with a ranking Commission official to discuss methodology. I was granted one within the hour, which is itself the fastest bureaucratic response I have received on any world I have surveyed.

The official was polite, warm, articulate, and told me absolutely nothing of substance in forty minutes of conversation. I left with the distinct impression that I had been handled rather than answered.

> **Corvid:** the theories about Cantho's governance that circulate in the present era — an unchained machine intelligence, a captive circle of precognitive minds, something considerably stranger wearing a very good disguise — are, I want to stress, theories, and nothing in this surveyor's notes proves any of them. But I notice that nobody, then or now, has ever produced the alternative: a straightforward answer.
>
> **Two-Coin:** "Handled rather than answered" is exactly the phrase I'd have used, and I wasn't even in the room. That's the part that gets me.

## From a Public Notice, Cantho Central Registry

The Central Planning Commission is pleased to announce expanded honors and stipends for citizens demonstrating exceptional aptitude in pattern-recognition, probabilistic reasoning, and related cognitive disciplines. Interested citizens are invited to register for assessment at any Commission office. All assessed citizens, regardless of outcome, will receive a commemorative token and the Commission's sincere gratitude for their civic participation.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** I want very much to believe this notice means exactly what it says, and nothing more. I have not managed it.
>
> **Two-Coin:** "Regardless of outcome" is the phrase I'd cut out and pin to a wall somewhere as a warning to myself.
>
> **Cinder:** I wouldn't register if they paid me in [[the-porth-belt|cherambilth]]. "Regardless of outcome" means they keep whatever they measured about you either way. A commemorative token is a cheap price for somebody else's permanent record of exactly how your mind works.
>
> **Two-Coin:** For once I'm with him instead of rolling my eyes at him. That notice reads friendlier than it should.
