## Overview

*Mandate-era summary.* Governed by the Central Planning Commission, an unusually efficient civilian administration responsible for infrastructure, resource allocation, and dispute arbitration. No irregularities found on audit. Surveyor recommends routine reassessment only; no cause for elevated classification.

## Field Notes

> **[FIELD NOTE — Factor Beniamin Curro, independent grain trader, 2610.204]**
> I will say this for Cantho: I have never in my career had a contract dispute resolved so quickly, so fairly, or so completely to my satisfaction. The Central Planning Commission's arbitration office settled a six-month disagreement with a [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Hypsila]] supplier inside of two hours, cited precedent I'd never heard of, and produced a settlement both parties found genuinely reasonable. I do not entirely trust it, and I could not tell you why.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Surveyor-Adept Yuen Petrakis, Terran Mandate Cartographic Office, 2609.033]**
> Assessment: Cantho's Central Planning Commission governs with an efficiency that borders on the uncanny — infrastructure maintained ahead of schedule, resource allocation with no waste I could detect in three weeks of auditing, public disputes resolved before, in one case, the dispute had been formally filed. I requested an audience with a ranking Commission official to discuss methodology. I was granted one within the hour. The official was polite, warm, articulate, and told me absolutely nothing of substance in forty minutes of conversation. I left with the distinct impression that I had been handled rather than answered.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Cor. Dassin, Cerberus Corps field agent, 3188.168]**
> "Efficient" is the word every good government uses for itself right before somebody finds out what the efficiency actually cost, and who paid it. The theories about Cantho's present-day governance — an unchained machine intelligence, a captive circle of precognitive minds, something stranger wearing a very good disguise — are theories, and I want to stress that nothing in the old survey proves any of them. But nobody, then or now, has ever produced the alternative: a straightforward answer.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Nine-Finger Sable, smuggler (self-described), 3140.302]**

## Traveler's Notes: Cantho, Present Day

*Practical addendum, mostly neutral.* Cantho remains an easy, pleasant stop for anyone not inclined to think too hard about how easy and pleasant it is. Dining and lodging near the harbor district are genuinely good and reasonably priced, and the Commission's arbitration office will resolve a contract dispute faster and more fairly than anywhere else in the sector — see the older field note above; nothing about that has changed.

Entertainment is plentiful, licensed, and unremarkable — concert halls, public gardens, a respectable gaming quarter — all of it running with the same quiet efficiency as everything else here. Company and companionship services operate through registered houses only; unregistered arrangements are not illegal so much as simply unheard of, which most visitors find stranger than any law would be.

**On bribes:** don't bother. Customs and harbor fees are fixed, published, and collected exactly as posted, and every attempt to pay "a little extra to move things along" is met with the same polite confusion — not refusal, confusion, as though the concept hadn't quite been anticipated. Frequent visitors report this is more unsettling than a straightforward no would be.
