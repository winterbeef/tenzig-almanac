## From the Shipping Register of the Tabet-Perope Freight Consortium

Perope system: three rocky worlds unfit for settlement, a modest asteroid works, and a star that every instrument reading we've taken says is exactly as boring as it looks. Freight runners cross it eleven times a day without incident and without escort. The Freight Consortium petitioned twice to have the "hazard surcharge" removed from Perope crossings entirely, on the grounds that there has been no recorded hazard in a generation. Both petitions were denied for reasons that were not explained to us.

> **Corvid:** I have read this entry perhaps forty times, and it does not get any easier. Perope today is not crossable without a hydrogen-shielded hull and an escort from the Guild of Pilots, full stop, and has not been for six hundred years. Whatever changed Perope's star into what it now is, it changed fast enough, or quietly enough, that a freight consortium's ledgers show no warning of it at all.
>
> **Two-Coin:** I've run that crossing four times with the Guild's own escorts and I still don't sleep right the night after. Whatever "reasons that were not explained" the surcharge board had, I'd put money they knew something and weren't telling a freight consortium's bookkeepers.
>
> **Cinder:** "Reasons that were not explained" — try telling that line to any dockworker who's ever had a safety complaint waved off by the same office cashing the hazard surcharge. I don't believe for a second nobody on that board knew anything. I believe somebody decided a freight consortium's questions weren't worth an honest answer, and that's a different failure entirely, and a much more familiar one.
>
> **Two-Coin:** Could be both. Doesn't have to be a conspiracy to also be a cover-up.

## From the Log of Master Rigger Halvor Oduya, Independent Salvage

Picked up strange traffic out toward the second rock today — a big hull, civilian registry markings but naval-grade shielding, moving slow and deliberate like she's got somewhere specific to be and no hurry getting there. Hailed her twice, got a polite refusal of contact both times, very professional, very final. My second says she looks like she's put a long transit behind her already — hull scoring, drive wear, the kind of wear you don't pick up on a short hop. Asked around dock-side after. Nobody's talking, which in my experience means somebody's paying them not to.

> **Corvid:** I believe — though I cannot prove it, and would not swear to it before a Steward of anything — that Master Oduya watched a very particular vessel pass through this system, not stop and not start here, but pass through on its way somewhere else. Where she came from before that is a question this entry does not answer, and a question, frankly, nobody has ever answered to my satisfaction. I have gathered what little the record offers on that question in [[the-unmarked-departure|its own entry]], later in this volume.
>
> **Two-Coin:** For the record, "somebody's paying them not to talk" is doing a great deal of work in that log entry, and I don't love it.
>
> **Cinder:** She'd already put a transit behind her that nobody dockside could account for, and "nobody's talking" because somebody was paying them not to. That's not mystery. That's a cover-up with better lighting. I think less about where she came from than about who was cutting the checks to keep it quiet.

## From a Warning Posted at the Tabet Docks, Author Unrecorded

TO ALL CREWS BOOKING PASSAGE THROUGH PEROPE:

The [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Eurymedon Concern]] has purchased exclusive survey and salvage rights to the second rock and its surrounding debris field for a period of no less than eleven years. Unauthorized approach will be treated as trespass on Concern property and reported to the appropriate naval authority.

Crews are reminded that "appropriate naval authority" in this instance means the Concern's own security contractors, who are, in the Consortium's professional opinion, considerably less patient than the actual navy.

*[hand unknown]*

> **Corvid:** The Eurymedon Concern's business in this system, and what became of it, and what grew — much later, much changed — from its ashes, deserves better than a passing note here. See [[eurymem-and-hypsila|Eurymem and Hypsila]].
>
> **Two-Coin:** Eleven years is a long time to sit on one rock and not tell anybody why. I've bought silence before. It's never for eleven years unless the thing you're hiding is either enormous or enormously embarrassing.
