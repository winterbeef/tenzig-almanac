## Field Notes

> **[FIELD NOTE — Tabet-Perope Freight Consortium shipping register, 2611.045]**
> Perope system: three rocky worlds unfit for settlement, a modest asteroid works, and a star that every instrument reading we've taken says is exactly as boring as it looks. Freight runners cross it eleven times a day without incident and without escort. The Consortium petitioned twice to have the "hazard surcharge" removed from Perope crossings entirely, on the grounds that there has been no recorded hazard in a generation. Both petitions were denied for reasons that were not explained to us.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Sella Voth, Guild of Pilots escort captain, 3151.290]**
> I've run that crossing more times than I can count with a full hydrogen shield and a two-ship escort, and I still don't sleep right the night after. Whatever "reasons that were not explained" the old surcharge board had six hundred years back, I'd put money they knew something and weren't telling a freight consortium's bookkeepers. Something changed here, and it didn't leave a note.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Cor. Dassin, Cerberus Corps field agent, 3188.166]**
> Filed for the record: "reasons that were not explained" is a phrase I've read in a dozen Mandate-era files now covering a dozen different systems, always right before something in that system stopped behaving the way the file said it would. I don't think the old surveyors were lying. I think somebody above them decided a freight consortium's questions weren't worth an honest answer. Different failure. Same result, six centuries later.

## Log, Master Rigger Halvor Oduya, Independent Freight

Picked up strange traffic out toward the second rock today — a big hull, civilian registry markings but naval-grade shielding, moving slow and deliberate like she's got somewhere specific to be and no hurry getting there. Hailed her twice, got a polite refusal of contact both times, very professional, very final. My second says she looks like she's put a long transit behind her already — hull scoring, drive wear, the kind of wear you don't pick up on a short hop. Asked around dock-side after. Nobody's talking, which in my experience means somebody's paying them not to.

## The Derelict

Sometime after those old surveys were filed, something took up residence out past the second rock — or was there all along and only got noticed once enough people started asking why this crossing needed a full hydrogen shield to feel safe. A dead hull sits dark in that stretch of the system now, colony-ship scale, and nobody currently working the corridor remembers a time before it. *Compiler's note: this is very likely the same unlogged vessel described in [[the-unmarked-departure|the entry on her original departure]], her last known heading, and her present resting place, though nobody in this volume has stated that connection outright.*

Whatever else it is, the wreck throws a shadow that matters in more than the visual sense. Ships holding close to its bulk read markedly lower radiation counts than anywhere else in the system — enough of a difference that it's stopped being a curiosity and started being a service.

> **[FIELD NOTE — Sella Voth, Guild of Pilots escort captain, 3195.208]**
> Used to be the whole job on this crossing was "bring a good shield and hope." These days the Guild runs a second contract most crews don't ask about until they need it: downwell escort, in toward the wreck's shadow, for anyone whose own shielding won't hold the full transit. Costs more than the standard corridor rate. Worth every credit. I ask every client what they actually want once we're in close, because "just the shelter" and "a closer look" are two very different jobs, and only one of them is one I'll take.

## Traveler's Notes: Perope, Present Day

*Practical addendum, mostly neutral.* Perope has no population and no starport, so there's nothing here in the way of bars, entertainment, or company — it's a crossing, not a destination, and travelers should plan their liberty stops for [[avire|Avire]] or [[tabet|Tabet]] on either side of it instead. The one thing worth knowing about is the derelict — see above — and most crews would rather not know more than that.

What Perope does have is a cost of passage. Guild of Pilots escort runs the standard corridor rate, adjustable by cargo class and how nervous your captain is; independent crews report the Guild's captains rarely negotiate the base fee but will often waive incidentals for a regular client. A separate, pricier downwell contract will run you in close to the wreck's radiation shadow if your own shielding isn't up to the crossing on its own — ask the Guild office directly, since not every captain openly advertises taking that job. Running the corridor unescorted is legal, common among freight haulers, and, per more than one escort captain in this volume, not something they'd personally recommend, though none of them can point to a specific reason why beyond a feeling they don't enjoy discussing.

There is no customs presence and nothing resembling a bribe to pay — there is no one out here to pay it to.
